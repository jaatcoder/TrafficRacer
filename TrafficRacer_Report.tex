\section{Abstract}
This document describes the architecture, key modules, and flow of the Traffic Racer game. It includes a vertical, decorative flowchart, implementation notes, and an appendix listing project scripts and assets to help writing a final report.

\section{Design Goals}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Smooth, infinite city streaming to simulate an endless road.
  \item Lightweight AI traffic with deterministic lane movement.
  \item Responsive player controls and camera smoothing.
  \item Clear state transitions (menu, gameplay, game-over) with audio feedback.
\end{itemize}

\section{Module-Level Descriptions}
Below are concise descriptions of the primary scripts and their responsibilities. Use these in your report subsections and for code references.

\subsection{Environment}
\textbf{EndlessCity.cs} --- Manages instantiation and recycling of city blocks to create an illusion of an infinite world. Uses pooling where possible to limit allocations.

\subsection{Traffic / AI}
\textbf{CarSpawner.cs} --- Spawns AI vehicles at lane entry points, randomising prefab type and spawn timing based on difficulty.

\textbf{TrafficManager.cs} --- Coordinates global traffic density and difficulty scaling as the player progresses.

\subsection{Player 
\& Controls}
\textbf{CarController.cs} --- Reads input, applies forces/torques to the player vehicle, and clamps speed/steering.

\textbf{LaneMovement.cs} --- Used by AI and optionally by player vehicles to snap to lane centers and perform lane changes smoothly.

\subsection{Collision \& Feedback}
\textbf{DestroyOnContact.cs} --- Triggered on collision to apply damage, spawn FX, and signal the game-over state.

\subsection{UI \& Audio}
\textbf{UIManager.cs} \& \textbf{GameOverPanelAnimation.cs} --- Manage HUD updates and animated transitions for game over.

\textbf{AudioManager.cs} \& \textbf{CarSound.cs} --- Play background music and per-vehicle sound fx.
